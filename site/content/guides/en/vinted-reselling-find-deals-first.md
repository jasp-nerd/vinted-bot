You can resell on Vinted without paying for a bot. What you need is a short list of searches you know well, a clear idea of your margin after buyer protection and postage, and alerts that reach you within minutes of a listing appearing. A free alert tool covers that. Paid bots mostly add autobuy, which costs a lot and puts your Vinted account at risk.

This guide is about finding stock on Vinted. It isn't a get-rich plan, and it won't claim one: resale margins are thin and depend on your time.

## Do the margin maths first

The most common beginner mistake is to compare the listed price with what something sells for elsewhere. The listed price isn't what you pay.

What you pay on Vinted:

- The item price.
- Buyer protection, which in most countries is a fixed amount (€0.70, or £0.70 in the UK) plus 5% of the item price, according to Vinted's help pages at the time of writing.
- Postage, which depends on the carrier and parcel size.

What you keep when you resell:

- The sale price.
- Minus the selling fee of the platform. On Vinted, private sellers pay no selling fee (the buyer pays buyer protection). Other platforms take a cut, often around 10% or more.
- Minus postage if you pay it.

A worked example: a jacket listed at €25. Buyer protection is €0.70 plus €1.25, so €26.95 before postage. Add, say, €4 postage: €30.95. If it resells on Vinted for €45 with the buyer paying postage, you keep €45, a margin of about €14. On a platform with a 10% fee and you paying €5 postage, you keep €35.50, a margin of under €5. Same jacket, very different deal.

That's why your search's price limit should be the total you'll pay, not the listed price. [The buyer protection fee explained](/guides/vinted-buyer-protection-fee/) has more examples.

## Which categories flip, and which are a race

Resellers tend to focus on the same things: branded menswear (Ralph Lauren, Stone Island, Carhartt, Arc'teryx), trainers, games and consoles, phones and electronics, and trading cards. These sell fast, which is exactly why they're crowded. A €15 Switch game listed under value can be gone in under a minute, often to someone running autobuy.

You'll usually do better in a niche you know better than most buyers:

- Brands that aren't famous but resell well, which you only learn by handling stock.
- Sizes most resellers ignore, such as large or small sizes in good brands.
- Bundles and job lots, where the seller prices the lot rather than each item.
- Badly titled or misspelled listings, which most searches miss.
- Collectibles where knowledge matters: card sets, figure lines, vintage tags.

## Search recipes that find underpriced listings

### Search the typo, not just the brand

Sellers misspell brands all the time: "Carhart", "Ralph Loren", "Arcteryx", "Levis". Each common misspelling can be its own search, or one search with alternatives (`carhartt|carhart`).

### Use exclusions to cut noise

Add words to skip such as "fake", "replica", "inspired", "style", "box only", "for parts", "damaged". Those cost you time on every alert.

### Price below typical, not just below a number

A fixed limit is a blunt tool. vinted-bot learns the typical total price of what each search finds and marks listings that are clearly under it (it needs 15 finds in two weeks before it knows). You can choose to be told only about those.

### Let it calculate your margin

In vinted-bot's search editor there's a resale section: enter what the item resells for, the selling fee (0 for Vinted, about 10 for eBay) and your postage, and each alert shows what you'd keep. Set a minimum margin and anything that leaves too little isn't sent.

### Watch sellers, not only searches

Some sellers consistently underprice: house clearances, parents emptying wardrobes, people moving abroad. Follow them, and get an alert for everything they upload.

### Cover more than one country

The same search on another Vinted country site shows a different mix of sellers. One vinted-bot search can watch several, and a listing seen twice is announced once.

## Setting up free alerts

vinted-bot is free and open source. It runs on your own computer, checks each search every minute and sends new listings to Discord, Telegram or your phone with the total price, the seller's rating and Buy and Message buttons. It never logs in to Vinted.

1. Install it with the one-line command from the homepage.
2. Paste each search link, or build the searches in the dashboard.
3. Set price limits as totals, add words to skip, and fill in the resale section if you want margins.
4. Send alerts somewhere you'll see them fast: a Discord channel per category, or a Telegram group shared with a partner. Guides for [Discord](/guides/vinted-discord-alerts-webhook/) and [Telegram](/guides/vinted-telegram-alerts/).

## Speed: what alerts can and can't do

Vinted adds new uploads to its search a few minutes after they're posted. Every tool, paid or free, only sees the listing then. vinted-bot checks every minute, so you usually hear within a few minutes of the upload. In a quiet niche that's plenty. On a hyped item that everyone watches, autobuy often gets there first.

Some practical habits help more than a faster bot:

- Have your payment method and address saved in Vinted, so checkout is a couple of taps.
- Use the Buy button in the alert, which opens checkout directly, instead of the listing.
- Turn on sound only for the alerts that matter (the deal tiers), so you react to those.

[Why you're never first on Vinted](/guides/why-youre-never-first-on-vinted/) covers this in more depth.

## The rules: Vinted, tax and your account

Vinted is built for people selling their own things. If you buy to resell as a regular activity, Vinted expects you to sell through a business account, and in most countries a regular buying-to-resell activity counts as trading, with registration and tax obligations. Under the EU's DAC7 rules, Vinted reports sellers with 30 or more sales, or €2,000 or more, in a year to the tax authorities. The UK has its own equivalent. This isn't legal advice; check your country's rules.

On bots specifically: tools that buy, message or relist for you need your login, and Vinted restricts accounts it suspects of automation. For a reseller, the account with its reviews is the business. An alert tool that never logs in keeps it out of the picture. More in [are Vinted bots allowed?](/guides/are-vinted-bots-allowed/).
